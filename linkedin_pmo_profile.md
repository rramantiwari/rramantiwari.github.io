## 10. "Why Technical PMO?" Interview Narrative & Battle Cards

> 💡 *Use these concise talking points when interviewers ask about your transition from Senior Engineer to Technical PMO Lead.*

### 1. "Tell me about yourself and your pivot to PMO."
> *"Over the past 6+ years, I started as a hands-on software engineer building scalable backends and enterprise architectures. As I progressed into senior leadership, I realized the biggest risk to software projects is rarely raw coding ability—it's delivery governance, unclear requirements, unmanaged dependencies, and communication breakdown between business stakeholders and developers.*  
> *I stepped into Technical PMO leadership because I bring a unique dual advantage: I have the discipline to establish PMO standards, RAID logs, and executive RAG dashboards, but unlike traditional administrative PMOs, I can read the codebase, evaluate technical architecture, challenge engineering estimates realistically, and earn the deep respect of engineering squads."*

### 2. "How do you handle scope creep and tight client deadlines?"
> *"I enforce a strict Change Request (CR) workflow backed by data. When a stakeholder requests a mid-sprint change, I don't just say 'no'—I present the trade-off matrix: the impact on sprint velocity, the risk to current release milestones, and alternative phase-two deployment options. By visualizing the impact transparently in Jira and our RAG report, stakeholders make informed business decisions rather than reactive demands."*

### 3. "How do you manage cross-functional dependencies?"
> *"I maintain an active Dependency & RAID matrix. For instance, on multi-tier projects involving frontend, backend, and external APIs (like payment gateways or government registries), I map API contract milestones early. If a third-party API or downstream squad is delayed, we use mock contracts and stubs to prevent blocking frontend delivery. We track these weekly in PMO gating sessions to catch risks before they become roadblocks."*
