## 7. TPM Interview "Battle Cards" & Framing for Your Next Job

When interviewing for TPM roles at your next organization, you will encounter 4 standard questions. Use these battle-tested narratives:

### 1. "You have a solid software development background. Why do you want to be a TPM?"
> *"Throughout my career, I found that the success or failure of software projects is almost never determined by raw coding capability—it is determined by delivery governance, requirement clarity, dependency management, and communication.*  
> *As a Technical Project Manager, I bring the best of both worlds: I have the organizational rigor to manage sprint cadences, RAID logs, and executive RAG dashboards, but because I have built scalable enterprise systems myself, I can evaluate technical trade-offs, challenge engineering estimates realistically, and earn immediate credibility with the engineering team. I don't just track tickets—I unblock engineers and guarantee business outcomes."*

### 2. "How do you handle an engineer who says a task will take 3 weeks when you believe it should take 3 days?"
> *"I never confront an engineer with arbitrary doubt. Instead, I conduct a structured technical breakdown: 'Walk me through the components—what are the database migrations, third-party API dependencies, testing edge cases, or legacy code constraints?'*  
> *Usually, one of two things happens: either there is hidden complexity (like unmanaged technical debt or compliance requirements) which I can now factor into the risk log, or the engineer was over-engineering the solution. Together, we carve out an MVP sprint milestone that ships 80% of the value in 3 days, followed by iterative enhancements."*

### 3. "How do you handle scope creep when an executive or client asks for urgent changes mid-sprint?"
> *"I practice 'data-driven trade-offs' rather than saying an outright 'no'. I evaluate the request against current sprint velocity and create a transparent impact analysis: 'We can incorporate this new feature in this sprint, but it means we must deprioritize Task A or move the release date by 4 days. Which option aligns best with our quarterly business KPI?'*  
> *By making the trade-off visible in Jira and our RAG report, stakeholders understand the cost of scope changes and make informed business choices."*

### 4. "How do you manage cross-functional dependencies across multiple squads?"
> *"I maintain an active Dependency Matrix during sprint grooming. If our squad depends on an external API or another team's database schema, I establish an API Contract (mock endpoints and data schemas) in Week 1. This allows both teams to develop concurrently without blocking each other. I track dependency health in our weekly Scrum of Scrums, so any delay is caught and mitigated 2 weeks before release, not the day before deployment."*
